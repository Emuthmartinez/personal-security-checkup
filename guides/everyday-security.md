# Everyday protections when relevant

Use this reference only when the person's situation makes a step useful. Keep the [core skill's](../SKILL.md) one-action format, recovery checks, action-specific consent and private verification. Do not turn it into another intake inventory. Official provider instructions and [source notes](sources.md) support the next action; verify current behavior before changing it.

| Situation | One useful next step | Check and stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unexpected urgent request, invoice, login prompt or “support” call | Open the service independently or call an already known contact using a different trusted route. Treat familiar voices, caller ID, QR codes and messages as unverified. Never approve a sign-in the person did not initiate or install remote access for an unsolicited caller. | Confirm through the independent route before payment, sharing or access. If money moved or access was granted, use [incident help](incidents.md). Do not ask the agent to test a suspicious link with personal credentials. |
| Public profile, shared file/calendar, photo or live location exposes more than intended | Review one audience or active sharing permission; consider discoverability and location metadata where supported. Verify an approved audience reduction using a safe non-sensitive view. | Check affected recipients and dependencies first. Removing a share does not recall copies. For coercion/stalking, changes can alert someone: use a safer device and specialist help before proceeding. |
| An app, extension or connected integration has unnecessary access | Check its purpose, publisher and actual permissions through official settings; choose one unneeded grant for explicit revocation. Prefer less access when it still meets the person's need. | Read back that grant's state. Removal may break a needed workflow and does not prove previously collected data was deleted. Do not grant broad mailbox/device access just to inspect it. |
| Unused account, email address or phone number | Check subscriptions, purchases, stored records, shared ownership, linked sign-ins and recovery dependencies before proposing retirement. Move and test dependencies privately first. | Export only what the user needs privately; explain retention/deletion limits and any irreversible loss. Closure, subscription cancellation and data erasure are separate actions. No automatic deletion from an inactivity list. |
| Backup exists only as sync or on a permanently connected device | Review the provider's recovery/versioning and account recovery; consider a separate protected/offline copy for important data where practical. Test one harmless restore with approval. | Keep keys private and verify the restored file. Sync on or a recent backup timestamp alone is not recovery evidence. Do not disconnect/delete the only usable copy or wipe suspected malware before preserving necessary evidence. |
| Shared, borrowed or public device | Use a private profile/device for sensitive accounts when available; avoid saving passwords or personal passkeys to someone else's credential store. Review only the user's own sessions with scope. | A private-browsing window does not establish device trust. Keep recovery available before signing out; do not inspect other household members' accounts. Managed devices go to their administrator. |
| Router, camera or smart-home device matters to privacy | Check supported firmware updates, administrator access and whether remote access is actually needed. Research the manufacturer's official route for one device. | Plan reconnection, household safety and recovery before changes; do not reset a router, disable a safety device or remove another person's access automatically. Unsupported devices may need a replacement plan. |
| Loss/incapacity would leave important access stranded | Review whether a personally chosen trusted contact or supported emergency-access mechanism is suitable, explaining exactly what access it grants and when. | The person keeps recovery material privately; avoid secret-sharing in chat or an agent-held master copy. Consent from affected people and provider rules still apply. |

Avoid recommending a paid VPN, antivirus, identity service or hardware purchase simply to complete a checklist. Establish the actual threat, existing protections, supported platform behavior, cost and recovery burden first. No single product makes unsafe links, compromised devices or identity exposure harmless.
