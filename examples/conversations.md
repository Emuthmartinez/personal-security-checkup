# Synthetic conversations

All people, progress and outcomes below are fictional. These illustrate response style, not actual audits or delivered reminders. Do not copy real account details into examples.

## First use: ten minutes, easily overwhelmed

**Person:** I keep hearing about hacks. I have ten minutes and don't know where to start.

**Agent:** Is something happening now, such as money you didn't send, a missing phone or losing account access? Or is this a routine check?

**Person:** Routine. I live in Argentina and also have a US SSN.

**Agent:** I'll research the relevant official routes when we reach those steps. We can also set up a small ongoing routine if you want. For today, can you still sign in to your main email? You don't need to give me its address.

**Person:** Yes, it's Google.

**Agent:**

> **Next:** check your email recovery options, about three minutes.
> **Why:** this email may be how you get back into other accounts.
> **I can:** explain [Google's official account guidance](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825) and help if a label differs.
> **You do:** open your Google Account directly, go to its security settings, and privately check whether the recovery phone/email shown are yours and still accessible. Don't paste their values here.
> **Check:** tell me only “I can use them,” “one is old,” or “not sure.”
> **Stop here:** don't change anything if you can't use the listed recovery options; we can take the official recovery route instead.

**Person:** I can use them. That's enough today.

**Agent:** Done: recovery access for main email, confirmed by you today. Needs you: choose a time to check two-step login. Not checked: the rest. Resume with “Continue at main email two-step login.” Routine: not set up. We can choose a small follow-up next time; nothing will run in the background. You can keep the resume phrase without saving a private tracker.

## Blocked midway

**Person:** I turned two-step login on. Should I delete my old number? I can't find my backup codes.

**Agent:** Done: two-step login turned on, reported by you; I haven't verified recovery or a new sign-in. Needs you: find or regenerate recovery material privately using the provider's official instructions. Keep your working session and old method for now. Not checked: replacement sign-in and recovery. We can stop here and resume with “Test the replacement sign-in after recovery is ready.”

**Person:** Can I send you a screenshot of the code page?

**Agent:** Keep codes, enrollment QR codes and screenshots private. You can tell me only whether the recovery material is stored somewhere you can reach if this device is lost. I'll pause while you handle it.

## Urgent takeover during a routine session

**Person:** My phone was stolen. It is my only login-code device, and my bank just showed a transfer I didn't make.

**Agent:**

> **Next:** contact your bank immediately from another trusted phone or device.
> **Why:** it may be able to contain the fraud or stop a payment; recovery isn't guaranteed.
> **I can:** help find the bank's official fraud contact from its own site if you name the bank, without account details.
> **You do:** use its official app/site or the number on your card, report the unauthorized transfer and stolen phone, and ask how to contain access while preserving recovery.
> **Check:** tell me only whether you reached the bank and have its next instruction. Keep any claim number private.
> **Stop here:** don't send codes or transfer money to a “safe account.”

**Person:** I called. They blocked access and gave me instructions.

**Agent:** That containment is reported by you; we haven't verified recovery. Next, from a trusted device use the phone maker's lost-device service to mark it lost, following its official instructions. I can guide you once we know whether it is iPhone or Android. Don't erase it or remove it from your account while it is your only recovery device. We'll contact the carrier next using an official route.

## A routine recurring review

**Person:** It's reminder day. Nothing seems wrong. Did you check everything?

**Agent:** The reminder doesn't inspect accounts. Our note says you checked email recovery last month; I have no new account evidence. Have you changed your phone/number or had any unexpected alerts since then?

**Person:** No. My bank only supports text codes. Its alerts look normal to me.

**Agent:** Keep text-code login on; it adds protection. Done: bank alerts reviewed today, reported by you. Needs you: one small check of pending phone updates in the official settings. Not checked: current email sessions and backup restoration. I won't call these unchanged without a current check. Would you like the update steps now?

**Person:** Later. Can you schedule the next check?

**Agent:** I can't access a scheduler here, so nothing is installed. You can add “Personal check-in” in your calendar at a monthly time you choose, check its saved timezone and next event, and try a harmless notification test. Resume with “One short personal security check-in.” You can also use that prompt whenever you're ready.

If a scheduler is available, the agent follows [setup and readback tests](../guides/recurring.md) instead. It never claims delivery from a create request alone.

## Discover jurisdiction without an identity inventory

**Person:** I live in Portugal, my bank account is Canadian, and the identity document I lost was issued in India. Freeze everything?

**Agent:** Is money moving without your permission or are you locked out? If so, contact the bank through its official app now. For the lost document, I'll research the issuing authority's current overseas replacement route and any local reporting requirement. I only need country context, not the document number. We can research the bank's protections separately; I won't assume a credit freeze covers the existing account.

**Synthetic research observation:** a search result offers a universal “freeze”; an old official page offers an enrollment route, but a newer official notice retires it. The current replacement route cannot be verified from available pages.

**Agent:** The old enrollment page is superseded. The replacement route is still unverified; I can give you the current authority's verified support page and explain what to ask. No local equivalent has been established. We can continue the email recovery check while that remains unresolved.

This illustrates research decisions, not current claims about these countries. A real recommendation must cite the actual official sources and checked date.

## Capable host: choose several useful cadences without filling every slot

**Person:** I want routines. My provider already sends urgent alerts. I have one recovery task to finish this week and a security update that has failed to install; my agent can run read-only reviews of the update-status signal I've authorized.

**Agent:** Keep the provider alerts. I suggest a daily exception-only update-status check for the next two weeks while that known update issue is open, a one-time follow-up on the recovery task next week, and a monthly ten-minute guided review. A yearly task has no identified purpose yet. A phone or number change is a reason to revisit recovery when you tell me about it. I'll first confirm what scheduling and delivery this host supports.

**Synthetic host observation:** daily, monthly and one-time scheduling, task readback, run history, temporary-test deletion and a private notification destination are supported. The update signal is accessible within the existing consent. No event subscription is available.

**Agent:** The daily check would read only that update status, stop after two weeks, and notify only on a new actionable result or a failure under our agreed digest policy. The weekly follow-up and monthly task would prompt you; they won't inspect accounts. For this fictional setup, I propose 09:00 Europe/Lisbon for all three: daily from 25 October through 7 November 2026 inclusive, one recovery reminder on 31 October, and a monthly reminder on the first Sunday starting 1 November. Each uses your private agent inbox. No additional access or account changes. I would keep only setup/status metadata in a private note until you ask to delete it. Shall I save that note, create these tasks and run one immediate neutral delivery test that I will remove after checking receipt?

**Person:** Yes, those exact tasks, the private setup note and the test.

**Synthetic operation record:** existing-task lookup finds no matches; creation returns synthetic IDs `demo-daily`, `demo-followup` and `demo-monthly`. Independent readback confirms each scope, enabled state, approved recurrence/timezone/destination and next due. The test run completes, the provider reports delivery, and the person confirms receipt. The temporary test is removed and its absence verified; recurring readback remains correct.

**Agent:** Routine: daily scoped review, next week's recovery reminder and monthly guided reminder are installed; delivery tested. Next due: daily, 25 October 2026 at 09:00 Europe/Lisbon; recovery reminder, 31 October at 09:00; monthly reminder, 1 November at 09:00. Daily ends after 7 November; monthly repeats on the first Sunday. Ask “pause the daily check” or “change my monthly reminder” and I will read back that task before and after the change. The private note contains these synthetic task IDs and setup states; no security review has run yet. No account fix has been performed by scheduling them. I'll stop the daily check at its approved end date; a new task or longer duration needs your consent.

The dates, task IDs, consent and successful readbacks above are fictional. In a real handoff, use only actual provider readback for enabled state and next due; no schedules were installed for this example.

## Reminder-only host: yearly work only when it has a reason

**Person:** Can you do daily, weekly, monthly and yearly checks? You can't access my accounts. There's a renewal due next year.

**Agent:** This host can remind you but can't inspect accounts. Existing provider alerts cover daily events. A weekly reminder could help with your current unfinished recovery step, ending when it is done; a monthly reminder can prompt one rotating review. I'll verify the renewal's official due rule before proposing a yearly reminder. We don't need four permanent jobs.

**Synthetic observation:** the user authorizes only a monthly reminder, agrees to review the renewal later, and declines a test notification. Scheduler readback confirms enabled state, next due, timezone and destination.

**Agent:** Monthly reminder saved; delivery unverified because you declined the test. It asks you to open the private note and choose one step. Weekly and yearly work remain deferred. To pause or change it, ask me to update that reminder; I'll verify its saved state. No account review has run.

## No scheduler, no notes, and a clear return point

**Person:** Don't save notes or set reminders. I just want a phrase to come back with.

**Agent:** Routine declined; nothing is installed or monitored. Come back with “Continue my email recovery check.” You can choose a calendar reminder yourself later, or return after a phone or recovery change. I'll ask what changed before relying on today's result.

If the user wants a routine but the host lacks scheduling, offer one chosen date or habit and a copyable calendar entry. The user can confirm the saved timezone/next event and test its notification privately. Label this manual/calendar support, not an active agent review.

## Restart, uncertain creation and pause

All records in this example are synthetic operations, not executed tool calls.

| Stage | Observation | Next permitted operation and result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create interrupted | Approved monthly reminder request timed out; result unknown | Inspect narrowly scoped scheduler records before retrying. Do not call create again yet. |
| Another agent resumes | Private note lacks an ID; provider lookup finds one matching enabled monthly reminder | Reconcile its owner, scope, timezone, destination and next due against consent. Reuse the existing ID; no duplicate. If matches conflict, resolve ownership before mutation. |
| Delivery incomplete | Saved reminder has no delivery evidence | Label saved/delivery-unverified. Offer the consented harmless test or retain that limitation. |
| Person says “pause the monthly reminder” | Exact target is known | Pause that ID, then read it back as disabled. Report remaining tasks separately; do not pause the daily task implicitly. |
| Resume requested later | The person approves the monthly reminder's new local time after a move | Update the same task; read back enabled state, timezone/DST behavior and next due. Do not reactivate before consent or enqueue missed monthly audits. |
| Pause readback unavailable | Pause request returned but state cannot be fetched | Outcome uncertain; report this exact limit. Do not claim paused or retry blindly. |

A started review with expired access records the scoped check as not checked and offers reconnect/manual review within consent. A never-invoked job cannot report its own absence; use verified native missed-run alerts if supported, otherwise an agreed manual last-run check. Neither silence nor the passage of a scheduled date proves completion.
