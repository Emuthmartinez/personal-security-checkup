# Other countries: template

Use this when the user lives in, or has ties to, a country without its own module. For each item, find the official source (a government domain or the company's own site), give the user the URL, and say plainly what you verified and what you couldn't. If a protection doesn't exist in that country, say so and give the closest real alternative. Never guess. Never use a "helper" or reseller site when an official one exists.

The user types every ID number, PIN, and password directly into the official site. Never in chat.

## 1. Credit bureaus and credit records
- Find out who keeps credit records: a central-bank registry, private bureaus, or both. Search the central bank's and data-protection authority's sites.
- Can people freeze or lock their credit file? Many countries have no freeze. If not, find: the free-report right (often under the data-protection law), any fraud alert or "lost/stolen ID" register that lenders must check, and how often the free report is allowed.

## 2. Tax agency
- Does the tax agency offer an identity PIN, a second factor, a security level, or a way to see who is authorized to act for you? Turn on the strongest option.
- Can someone file or change things in your name with just your ID number? If so, prioritize protecting that login.

## 3. National digital ID and government portal
- Name of the national ID app or portal, how to secure it (strong password, verified phone, two-step if offered), and where to see and close active sessions.
- How to report a lost or stolen ID card and get it flagged so it can't be used to open accounts.

## 4. Mobile carrier and phone number
- Does the carrier offer an account PIN, SIM-change protection, or port-out protection? What does a SIM swap or port need (in-store ID, biometrics, an SMS PIN)?
- Is there a national number to block a stolen phone's IMEI and line?
- Can you get a list of all lines registered in your name?

## 5. Banks and payment apps
- Which banks and wallets they use, the official app, and each one's: login second factor or token app, device list, transaction alerts, card freeze, and stolen-phone procedure.

## 6. Do-not-call registry
- Does one exist? Official URL, what it covers (calls, SMS, messaging apps), how long it takes to work.

## 7. Where to report fraud
- Police or prosecutor cybercrime unit, the bank/financial regulator's complaint process, the consumer-protection agency, and the data-protection authority.

## 8. Common local scams
- Search the government's and the cybercrime unit's warnings for the scams most common there right now (WhatsApp account takeover, fake bank calls, fake marketplace payments, etc.) and teach the top two or three.

## Monthly
- Whatever free credit-record check exists, bank alerts, and sessions in the government portal.
