# US person living abroad module

Load this if the user has a US Social Security number (or US bank, brokerage, or retirement accounts) but lives outside the US. Load it together with the module for the country they live in.

Why it matters: an SSN can be abused in the US even if you never set foot there. Someone can open credit, file a tax return, or claim benefits in your name, and you won't see the mail.

The user types their SSN, PINs, and passwords directly into the official site. Never in chat. This module is security guidance only, not tax or legal advice.

## 1. Credit freeze at all three US bureaus
- Free, and the strongest protection against new accounts in their name.
  - Equifax: https://www.equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-freeze/
  - Experian: https://www.experian.com/freeze/center.html
  - TransUnion: https://www.transunion.com/credit-freeze
- Try online first. Online identity checks often fail without a current US address or US credit history. If that happens, use mail or phone:
  - TransUnion accepts a written request with name, address, and SSN at TransUnion, P.O. Box 160, Woodlyn, PA 19094, or by phone at 888-909-8872. It suggests including one proof of identity and two proofs of current address (photocopies only). Source: https://www.transunion.com/credit-freeze/mail-or-phone
  - Equifax and Experian also accept mailed requests. Get the current mailing address and document list from each bureau's own freeze page before sending; include your foreign address and your most recent US address if you had one.
- International mail to the US can be slow or suspended from some countries. Use a tracked courier for anything with copies of ID, and never send originals.
- Save each bureau's login or freeze PIN in the password manager.

## 2. IRS Identity Protection PIN
- Anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify their identity can get one, even if they don't file US returns. It stops someone filing a tax return in their name. Source: https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin
- Best path from abroad: the IRS online account (https://www.irs.gov/account), IP PIN section of the profile. It needs online identity verification.
- If online verification fails: Form 15227 is only for adjusted gross income on the last return below $84,000 (single) / $168,000 (married filing jointly), needs a phone for a call-back, and the IP PIN then arrives by US mail. That may not work well with a foreign address. In-person verification is only at US Taxpayer Assistance Centers.
- It's a new PIN every calendar year. If you got it online, you retrieve it online each year (nothing is mailed).
- The IRS never asks for an IP PIN by phone, email, or text.

## 3. Social Security (SSA)
- Living outside the US, you can create or use a my Social Security account with an ID.me credential. Create it before a criminal does. Sources: https://www.ssa.gov/foreign/ and https://www.ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-03193.html
- If you already had an account before moving abroad, you need an ID.me wallet to use it from a foreign address.
- If you'll never use it, you can ask SSA for an Electronic Access Block instead. It blocks online and automated phone access for everyone, including you, until you contact SSA to remove it. SSA's international line is 1-855-522-6936 (7 AM–5 PM Eastern); local carrier charges may apply.
- SSA never threatens, demands payment, or asks for your SSN by phone, email, or text. Report SSA scams at https://oig.ssa.gov.

## 4. US bank, brokerage, and retirement accounts
- Turn on every alert: logins, new devices, transfers, changes to address, phone, or email, and any withdrawal.
- Use passkeys or an authenticator app where offered. If the institution only does SMS, see the phone-number section below.
- Check who is listed as a trusted contact or beneficiary.
- Know how to freeze each card from the app and save the institution's international collect number (usually on the back of the card or the website), because US toll-free numbers often don't work from abroad.

## 5. FBAR and US tax accounts (security only)
- If they file an FBAR, do it only at FinCEN's official site, https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov. The government doesn't charge to file. Emails or calls about "FBAR penalties" that ask for payment or a login are a common scam.
- Lock down any tax software or preparer portal accounts with a unique password and two-step login. Whether and what to file is a question for a tax professional, not this checkup.

## 6. US phone number vs local number for two-step codes
- Many US accounts send codes by SMS to a US number. If they keep a US line (a carrier plan, eSIM, or Google Voice), losing it can lock them out, and a hijacked one lets a thief reset accounts.
- Best: move each important US account to passkeys or an authenticator app, then SMS doesn't matter.
- If they keep the US line: set the carrier PIN and port-out protection, keep it active, and note which accounts depend on it in the tracker.
- If they switch codes to their local number: make sure that carrier line is protected too (see their country module).
- Google Voice numbers can be lost if unused for a long time. Use it regularly or don't rely on it for codes.

## 7. US mailing address and mail forwarding
- Whatever US address their banks, IRS, or SSA have on file (a relative, a mail-forwarding service, an old address) is where account letters, new cards, and fraud notices go. Make sure it's someone they trust, and that the person tells them about anything unexpected.
- If a mail-forwarding service is used, lock its account with a unique password and two-step login; it receives everything.
- Don't leave an old US address that someone else now lives at as the address of record.

## Report fraud

- Scams: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov
- Tax identity theft: start at https://www.irs.gov/identitytheft

## Monthly
- US account alerts reviewed; once a year: confirm the three freezes are still on and grab the new IP PIN when filing.
